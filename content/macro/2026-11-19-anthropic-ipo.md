Anthropic priced its IPO on 18 November at USD 83 per share, above the USD 70–78 range, valuing the company at about USD 1.5 trillion, and started trading on Nasdaq on 19 November.

**Why we held the sleeve.** The eight names fall into two groups. Direct links: Amazon and Alphabet are Anthropic's largest outside shareholders, and Alphabet, Microsoft and AMD take part in the IPO-linked placement, together with Broadcom and Micron, which we hold in the replica. AI proxies without a direct link: ASML (EUV lithography), Palantir (enterprise AI software), Meta (AI investment) and Alibaba (low-valued cloud and AI option).

**Why we sold at the open.** Our audit of 22 US technology IPOs (2007–2026, 39 IPO–partner pairs) found no significant pre-IPO outperformance of the affiliated stocks: −0.53% against the S&P 500 from the Monday open to the debut-day open, a 41% beat rate, 95% confidence interval from −1.4% to +0.3%. Holding until the debut-day close was worse (−0.85%, 32% beat rate). The sleeve was a time-boxed variance catalyst, not an alpha bet: at 30% it lifted the simulated bonus probability from 16% to 24% while staying inside our 4% risk limit. Selling at the open also avoided Alibaba's earnings that morning, for which options priced a move of about 7.5%.

**Oracle.** On the same day I recommended removing Oracle from the replica: its exposure to AI risk looked out of proportion to its business, a risk we do not want in a core that is meant to follow the index. Citigroup, the next largest stock, took its place.
